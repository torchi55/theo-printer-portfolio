HELIOFORM STATION functions as a weather station engineered for the extreme thermal conditions of the Arizona desert. Rather than resisting the climate, the structure actively interprets and expresses it through an exterior skin utilizing a tiled brick system where each individual unit is calibrated to its specific solar exposure.

Lighter, thicker bricks are positioned in zones of high heat gain to provide thermal mass and reflect the sun, while darker, thinner bricks are placed in places that receives little to no sun. The resulting thermal gradient moves across the facade like a topographic map across terrain, serving as a visual index of solar impact.

The architectural massing emerged from these same environmental parameters, as airflow, orientation, and radiation pressure dictated the form from the perimeter inward.

While traditional architecture often treats environmental stress as a liability to be mitigated behind insulation or glazing, this design utilizes heat and light as primary materials.

211 Materials Drawings

A study in material assembly for a custom rail-mounted ceramic brick facade — working through how each panel indexes to its steel sub-rail and how those rails transfer load back to the primary frame. The central challenge: resolving panel spacing, rail geometry, and weathering joints simultaneously against structural tolerances across two coordinated drawing sets.
