Helioform Station

A project by Theo Janeway,
Marcus Chan & Kairei Jones

HELIOFORM STATION is a weather station engineered for the extreme thermal conditions of the Arizona desert. Rather than resisting the climate, the structure interprets and expresses it through an exterior skin of tiled brick, each unit calibrated to its specific solar exposure.

Lighter, thicker bricks sit in zones of high heat gain to provide thermal mass and reflect the sun, while darker, thinner bricks occupy areas receiving little to no sun. The resulting thermal gradient moves across the facade like a topographic map across terrain, a visual index of solar impact.

The massing emerged from these same parameters. Airflow, orientation, and radiation pressure dictated the form from the perimeter inward, organizing internal programs around zones of shade, compression, and exposure. The environmental diagram and the physical architecture exist as a singular entity.

Where traditional architecture treats environmental stress as a liability to be mitigated behind insulation or glazing, this design uses heat and light as primary materials. The facade operates as a live record of atmospheric conditions, constantly updated by the sun.

211 Materials Drawings

A study in material assembly for a custom rail-mounted ceramic brick facade — working through how each panel indexes to its steel sub-rail and how those rails transfer load back to the primary frame. The central challenge: resolving panel spacing, rail geometry, and weathering joints simultaneously against structural tolerances across two coordinated drawing sets.
