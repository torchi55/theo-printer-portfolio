115 Almond

A house for two people planning to grow old in it. Pool, elevator, gym and a caretaker's suite — all onto a 15% slope.

Summer 2026 · Preliminary Design

Brief + Programme A1.00

The clients are an elderly couple building the last house they intend to move into. Their list was specific — a pool, a gym, an elevator, a hot tub off the upper deck, and a bedroom for a caretaker who can come and go without walking through the house. The site pushes back: 8,711 sq ft, zoned R-1-7.5, capped at 45% coverage, and falling roughly 15% from Almond Street down to the rear. The answer was to stop treating the slope as a problem and let the house sit on it in two levels. The living floor arrives at street grade; everything heavy, wet or private drops into the hillside below it and opens straight onto the pool terrace. An elevator ties the two together, so the plan still works on the day the stairs stop working.

Main Floor · 638 SF

One Room, Three Jobs

The plan doesn't label this space "living room" — it's titled Living + Dining + Music, because the clients asked for all three and there was never enough floor to separate them.

  • 01
    The Music Wall

    Guitars, banjo and mandolin hang on the one long wall with no glazing in it, above the record shelves. Instruments become the storage and the artwork at once — the cheapest way to give music its own room without building one.

  • 02
    Clerestory Loft

    A 322 sq ft loft breaks up through the roof above. Its band of high windows drops daylight into the middle of the plan, so the room reads bright without turning every wall into glass.

  • 03
    Deck Threshold

    Full-height glazing puts the dining table on the hinge between the room and the 546 sq ft deck. In summer the two are one space; in winter the glass is the view.

  • 04
    Aimed at the Valley

    Every opening on this floor faces the Ashland hills. Elevating the living floor to street grade is what buys the view over the neighbouring lot — the slope pays for itself.

Arkitek · Summer 2026

115 Almond is a new single-family residence in Ashland, Oregon. The brief reads like a wish list — pool, elevator, gym, hot tub, a room for a caretaker — but on a 15% slope with a 45% coverage cap, the real work was fitting all of it onto 8,711 square feet without the house turning into a wall along the street.

I worked on it as an intern at Arkitek. The main-floor arrangement came to me already set — kitchen here, living there. What I did was draw it: both floor plan sheets, the roof plan, and the full set of nine renderings, each keyed to a camera marker printed on the plans so the clients can look at a view and find exactly where they're standing.

It goes in front of them this week. Whether it survives that meeting is a different question.
